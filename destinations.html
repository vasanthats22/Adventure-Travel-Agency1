Practical Tips

Everything You Need To Know

Quick answers to the questions every traveller asks before booking.

The two golden windows for Himalayan trekking are March to May (spring) and September to November (autumn). Spring brings blooming rhododendrons and milder temperatures; autumn offers the clearest mountain views and most stable weather.

Avoid the monsoon months (June–August) for lower-altitude treks, though rain-shadow regions like Upper Mustang and Ladakh remain viable.

Read our full season-by-season guide for details.

Visa requirements vary by destination. Nepal offers visa-on-arrival for most nationalities. Tanzania uses an e-Visa system. Iceland is part of the Schengen Area — check your nationality's Schengen requirements.

We handle visa assistance for all bookings — see our visa support page.

Budget varies by destination. Roughly: Nepal — $40–70/day, Ladakh — $35–60/day, Tanzania — $200–400/day (safari), Iceland — $150–250/day.

All Stackly trips include guides, permits, accommodation, meals on trail, and internal transport. See detailed cost breakdown.

It depends on the trip. Beginner treks (like easy safari and short hill walks) need only basic fitness. High-altitude routes (like Everest Base Camp) require good cardio, strong legs, and mental stamina.

We recommend 6–8 weeks of preparatory hiking before any challenging trek. See our training guide.

Comprehensive travel insurance is mandatory on all Stackly trips. Your policy must cover high-altitude trekking (up to 6,000m), emergency evacuation, medical expenses, and trip cancellation.

We partner with World Nomads and can help you find the right policy.
